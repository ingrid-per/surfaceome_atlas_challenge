Global top 10 & reserve

ranks 11–15 are the reserve
Each gene enters once, through its best cancer type, and the list is ordered by specificity score S. At most two genes per protein family, so one large family cannot fill the list.

Raised across many cancer types

breadth, not score
Ordered by the number of cancer types the gene passes in, then by its median S across them. This is the one list that does not rank on S — a gene can lead it without being the most specific anywhere.
Treat this list with particular caution. Immune infiltration and stromal content are present in tumours of every kind, so ranking by breadth tends to surface genes carried by those cells rather than by tumour cells — several entries here are known immune or fibroblast proteins. Tumour purity is not accounted for anywhere in this analysis, and it bites hardest on this tab.

every setting below is one of — runs that actually ran
Six numbers decide this ranking, and we chose five of them. Each was proposed, argued and recorded before any result was seen — but a defensible choice is still a choice. So the whole selection was re-run under every combination of reasonable alternatives, and each gene was scored on how often it survives. Nothing here is recomputed as you click. The controls look up a stored run, so every list you can reach is one that executed and passed its checks.
